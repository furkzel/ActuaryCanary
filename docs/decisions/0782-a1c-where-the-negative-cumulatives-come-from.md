# 0782 — A1c: where the negative cumulatives and the extreme totals come from (R04)

## Context
Record 0779: VaR 99.5 % of all lines spreads 15.6 % and TVaR 99 % 227.7 % across seeds 1 to 20 (demo, 10,000). Stage 1 of A1c changes no program code; it finds the mechanism. Method: the four paid triangles the bootstrap receives in the demo run (seed 1) were captured once in a scratch run (17 s); the bootstrap was replayed on them from the same stream. A scratch proxy (each line's bootstrap from `generator(seed, *group)`, rescaled per origin to the selected unpaid of the run, summed at correlation 0, as M12 does) reproduces the 0779 table to every digit (all lines 15.64 % and 227.73 %; per line identical) in 4 s instead of 3.3 min, and was used to try the remedies before the seed script confirms them.

## What the data and the fit look like (MOTOR_TPL, 10 × 10, no cut; PROPERTY cut after column 3)
- **No negative observed increment** in either triangle: the data has no recovery or reversal in the paid triangles.
- **The scale parameter is large against the oldest origins.** MOTOR_TPL φ = 7.67e5 (PROPERTY 4.66e5). The two oldest origins are small (cumulative paid at column 7: 5.7 m and 3.0 m, against 58.7 m on the latest diagonal of 2024). The coefficient of variation of one fitted cell, √(φ/μ), is 1.0 to 5.4 by column (5.4 in column 7, where the fitted increments average 0.04 m); that of the pseudo cumulative of origins 2016 and 2017 at column 7 is 0.38 and 0.48.
- **The residual pool is wide** (53 residuals, −1,933 to 3,251 in units of √μ). A residual from a large cell put on a small cell gives `μ + r √μ < 0`: the share of negative pseudo cells is 4 to 40 % by column on MOTOR_TPL (40 % in column 7) and 7 to 59 % on PROPERTY.

## Where the negative cumulatives are (seed 1, 10,000 pseudo-triangles)
- MOTOR_TPL: 63.5 % of the pseudo-triangles have a negative cumulative value; for 57.3 % it first appears in column 0 (the single first cell of a small origin), 4.8 % in column 1, below 1 % later. PROPERTY 73.3 %, of which 60.8 % in column 0. Most of them sit in early columns that rest on nine link ratios and do no harm there; they matter in two places: the latest single cell of the youngest origin (the projection starts from it, decision 0612: a negative start gives the large negative totals, down to −147 bn over the 20 seeds), and the denominator of the thin late columns.
- Every zero or negative denominator (1 and 13) and every extreme factor (5 and 35) of MOTOR_TPL is in columns 7 and 8 (2 and 1 link ratios). The factor of column 8 is `C(2016, 9) / C(2016, 8)` of one pseudo-row: its range is −10.7 to 6.8 (fitted 1.050); that of column 7, from the two oldest rows, −2.6 to 2.9 (fitted 1.054), and −157 to 41 over the 20 seeds.

## Which cells make the extreme totals
The factor of a late column multiplies the projection of every younger origin, the largest included. Seed 1, largest raw total 5.55 bn (median 304 m; chain reserve 300 m; rescaled to the selected unpaid 9.94 bn): factors 1.70 and 6.28 in columns 7 and 8, which lift origin 2025 to a mean of 2.93 bn and 2024 to 1.59 bn. Over the 20 seeds the largest raw total reaches 487 bn (seed 15). The 27.0 bn of record 0779 was not reproduced on the code of `main` with seed 1 (5.55 bn); it is of the same kind. The rescaling to the selected unpaid (factor 2.4 on origin 2025) multiplies the outliers but does not make them.

## Mechanism, in one sentence
The refit takes the factor of a thin late column as a ratio whose denominator is the pseudo cumulative of one or two small old origins; because the resampled residuals are large against those cells, that cumulative can come near zero or below, the factor explodes, and it is applied to every younger origin. None of the items of decision 5 is the cause: the fitted model, the residuals, φ and the pool are as they should be for this data; the defect is in what the refit does with the pseudo-triangle.

## Which remedies the mechanism supports
- **(a)**, the rule of 0776 in the refit, removes column 8 (one link ratio) but not column 7 (two). Proxy: all lines 4.79 % and 45.4 % (the 0779 scratch number).
- **(b)**, a guard: the over-dispersed Poisson model has no negative increment, and the data shows none; a pseudo increment below zero is outside the model, created by moving a residual from a large cell to a small one. The smallest correct guard is a **floor of the pseudo increment at zero** (or at the observed increment of the cell where the data itself is negative, a recovery the data shows). It keeps every pseudo cumulative at or above zero, so a denominator can no longer cross zero and the youngest origin no longer starts from a negative value. Setting a negative cell to its fitted mean was tried as well and rejected: it moves the cell further (by |value| + μ) and removes dispersion the data supports (for information, proxy at 50,000: 2.03 % and 1.89 %; the difference to the floor is within the noise of 20 seeds and is not a reason to choose it). Proxy, (a) + floor: 4.96 % and 4.06 % at 10,000; the remaining spread falls as 1/√n, so it is simulation noise, not outliers.
- **(c)**: proxy, (a) + floor at 20,000: 2.73 % and 2.97 %; at 50,000: 2.14 % and 2.21 %. The seed script confirms these in stages 2 and 3.
