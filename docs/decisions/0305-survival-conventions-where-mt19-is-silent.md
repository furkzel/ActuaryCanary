# 0305 — Survival analysis: conventions chosen where MT19 is silent

## Context
MT19 1.0.0 fixes the estimators, the parameters, the failure modes and the tolerances. Building it meant choosing in places where the text leaves room. None of these changes a number the specification fixes; each is tested (`tests/methods/survival/`, `tests/modules/test_survival_run.py`). The owner may want some of them written into MT19 (listed at the end).

## Decision
Durations and events (`modules/survival_run.py`)
- The report date is `claims.report_date`; without `claims`, or for a claim without that date, it is the first valuation date, a month end. The closing is the first valuation date in a closed status (CLOSED or CLOSED_NIL). When `claims.close_date` is given, the claim was never open again after its first closing and the date is not after the cut-off, the day of `close_date` is used, because it is then the first closing. For a reopened claim `close_date` is the last closing (as the generator and CL08 read it), so the first closed valuation date is used, a month end. Every duration that uses a month-end date is flagged `approximate`, counted per group in `survival_groups.csv` (`month_end_dates`) and reported as an INFO diagnostic (MONTH_END_DATES) per segment.
- A claim with no closing up to the cut-off is censored at the cut-off. A claim reopened and open at the cut-off keeps its event (the first closing). Claims closed before they were reported (DV-CLM-05) and claims reported after the cut-off are left out and said so in the information lines of `claim_durations`.
- `event = closed_with_payment`: a first closing as CLOSED is the event; a first closing as CLOSED_NIL ends the observation without an event, at the date of that closing (the claim can no longer close with a payment). This is a censoring that depends on the outcome of the other closing kind; the register entry is the way to choose it and the assumption of independent censoring applies with that caveat.
- The size class at report is CV08's class of the incurred at the claim's first valuation: gross, reporting currency, value-date prices when the price index is loaded (the basis of `size_classes`), against the threshold CV08 gives the claim's segment and line at the cut-off; a claim with an event code is CATASTROPHE; a claim with no amount at its first valuation is UNKNOWN. The class at the cut-off is `claim_size_class` of CV08 and is offered only as a descriptive grouping of curves, named `size_class_at_cutoff (descriptive)` in the tables; it is not accepted as a covariate (the register refuses it).
- Time units: a month is 365.25 / 12 days and a year 365.25 days (CV04); the Cox coefficients do not depend on the unit.

Analysis (`methods/survival/analysis.py`)
- The analysis runs once per segment, with the register entries of the scope `SEGMENT/survival` (`event`, `covariates` as one text entry with the names separated by commas, `ties`, `confidence`, `time_unit`). A segment is the "group" of the failure modes: a segment with fewer than 20 events gets its curves and no Cox model; every Kaplan–Meier group (all claims, a line, a report year, a level of a covariate, a class at the cut-off) with fewer than 20 events gets the curve and a THIN_EVIDENCE warning.
- Curves are made for all claims of the segment, by line, by report year, by every other covariate of the model, and by the size class at the cut-off. A grouping whose only value is UNKNOWN (for example the line when `claims` is not loaded) is left out.
- Cox covariates are dummy variables per level. The reference level is the level with the most events (then the most claims, then the name). A level with no events is merged into the reference level and the note says so; a covariate with one level in the segment is not used; with no covariate left, or with collinear covariates, a coefficient beyond 20 in size (a hazard ratio of 5e8: the events are separated by a level) or no convergence, the model is not run and the reason is in `survival_cox_models.csv`, in the diagnostics (NO_COX_MODEL) and in the notes of the module.
- The partial likelihood, its gradient and its information matrix are exact for Efron and Breslow; scipy's trust-region Newton method (`trust-exact`) finds the maximum, three Newton steps polish it, and convergence means a Newton decrement (gradient' information⁻¹ gradient) of at most 1e-8. The overall Wald statistic is β' information β; the frozen R values of the Wald statistic are printed to four significant digits (14.25, 29.93, 29.84), so the test compares them to 0.005, the rest at 1e-5 relative.
- The interval of a percentile of the time to event is the set of times at which the log-log interval of the curve contains the level (Brookmeyer and Crowley): the lower limit is the first time the lower limit of the curve reaches the level, the upper limit the first time its upper limit does; a limit that never gets there is empty and the note says "beyond observed range".
- Information-only outputs (decision 0616): the Grambsch–Therneau test uses the Schoenfeld residuals with the Efron-weighted risk-set mean, g(t) = 1 − the Kaplan–Meier estimate just before t, d (Σ (g − ḡ)(V r)_j)² / (V_jj Σ (g − ḡ)²) per covariate and d u'Vu / Σ (g − ḡ)² globally; it equals a loop written from the formula on the ovarian data and has no R reference. The concordance is Harrell's C over the pairs in which the shorter observation is an event (a censoring at the time of an event counts as later, two events at one time are not compared, equal scores count one half); it equals the pairwise count in tests and the lung value of this code (0.6371) is not frozen. The baseline is the Breslow cumulative hazard; the table gives survival for the covariate patterns present.
- Diagnostics: THIN_EVIDENCE, NO_COX_MODEL, NON_PROPORTIONAL_HAZARDS (global Grambsch–Therneau p below 0.01), COHORTS_DIFFER (log-rank test across report years below 0.01: the assumption that cohorts are comparable), MONTH_END_DATES (INFO). `survival_cohorts.csv` lists the share censored of every report year next to that test (the assumption that censoring is independent of the time to close is checked by comparing these shares).
- Output sizes are limited for the files only, never for the estimates: at most 400 rows per curve (evenly spread event times, first and last always included, every row an exact point of the curve), 300 scaled Schoenfeld residuals per covariate, 40 times for each of the 30 largest covariate patterns of the baseline. Percentiles and tests use all event times.
- Not random: no seed, no `randomness` row.

## Consequences
- Proposed additions to MT19 for the owner to accept in a later amendment: the rule for the first closing with the close date of `claims`; the treatment of a nil closing under `closed_with_payment`; the reference level and the merging note; the convergence rule and the bound on a coefficient; the definition of the interval of a percentile; the tie rules of the concordance; the group of the failure modes being the segment; the limits on the rows of the files.
- A later reference item freezes the Grambsch–Therneau test, the concordance and the baseline from R (`cox.zph`, `concordance`, `basehaz`) and moves them out of information (0616).
